## Working Papers

### Investor Horizon and Information Choice: Do long-term investors matter for price informativeness

**Abstract:**  
I show that long-term and short-term investors play distinct and time-varying roles in improving stock market informational efficiency. The contribution of long-term investors to price informativeness increases over time, particularly after 2004 and among firms with low-quality information, while the contribution of short-term investors declines. Exploiting long-term investors’ exits as an exogenous shock to long-term ownership, I find that the exit of long-term investors with significant ownership stakes (greater than 5%) is associated with a subsequent decline in price informativeness. I develop a heterogeneous-agent model in which investors allocate information-acquisition resources across signals with different horizons. Long-term investors allocate relatively more resources to information about future asset fundamentals, whereas short-term investors devote relatively more resources to contemporaneous demand signals. Consistent with the model, firms with a larger share of long-term investors exhibit greater price informativeness. Overall, the findings highlight the important and increasingly prominent role of long-term investors in incorporating fundamental information into stock prices.
